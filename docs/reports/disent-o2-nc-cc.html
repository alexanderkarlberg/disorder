DISENT at O(αs²) for NC and CC

All of DISENT's O(αs²) ingredients now include γ/Z and W exchange, so projection-to-Born works for every NC and CC process up to NNLO. Photon exchange is bit-identical to the original DISENT. For photon, Z, CC e⁻ and CC e⁺ exchange, the O(αs²) event shapes agree with NNLOJET within statistics.

Step 1: photon re-derivation

Each photon ingredient was re-derived independently and matched to DISENT's own expressions at random DIS phase-space points: with FORM (helicity-resolved traces), with an explicit Dirac-spinor calculation, and for the one-loop part with the Bern–Dixon–Kosower amplitudes ported from MCFM. Each match is exact, for arbitrary CF and CA:

The cause was on my side. The one-loop absorptive parts produce T-odd terms that flip sign between LL and RR, and they cancel only in the full sum.   • No DISENT bug found in the photon ingredients. An early mismatch in E was my own error (a missing real part), confirmed by the independent spinor calculation.

Step 2: Z and W exchange

The same calculations, resolved in helicity, give the parity-violating parts. Their difference, same minus opposite lepton–quark helicity, multiplies the coupling C3; the sum reproduces the photon structure, which multiplies C2. Implementation:

  • MATFOR: the parity-violating q→qgg, D1 and E terms. For W exchange, E is replaced by its W-attachment classes (identical d quarks; and d u ū from an incoming u, where the W sits on different lines in the two amplitudes). The code is generated from FORM as optimised Fortran (src/disent_o2_trees.f).
  • CONTHR3 and SUBFOR: the parity-violating spin-correlated dipoles. The g → q q̄ initial-state dipole averages the quark and antiquark Borns.
  • VIRTHR: the parity-violating finite one-loop part (src/disent_virt3.f, the BDK amplitudes). T-odd terms, odd under reflection of the event, are dropped.
  • Neglected, as in the structure functions: terms proportional to the sum of the quark axial couplings (the Z on a second quark line or on a quark loop), which vanish for complete generations.

Pointwise subtraction test

The four-parton matrix element must equal the sum of the dipoles in every single-unresolved limit. The table gives |M/ΣS − 1| for the u-quark channel, symmetrised over the labels of the outgoing partons, with dipoles whose Born contains the unresolved parton left out. Removing the new parity-violating MATFOR term gives ratios of about 0.83 instead.

This test checks singular behaviour, not the finite parts of the dipoles. For example, undoing DISENT's earlier SUBFOR bug fix leaves it unchanged. The finite parts are covered by the comparison with NNLOJET below. The new spin-correlated dipole is consistent with its integrated counterpart: its azimuthal average equals half of MATTHR's parity-violating term exactly.

  • Sensitivity (negative control). CC e⁻ was rerun with the same seeds and the one-loop parity-violating term switched off. The distributions move by up to 5% (ρE, τzE) and 16% (BzE) at small values, and χ² against NNLOJET becomes 1568, 2623 and 10854 for 24 bins. The orange curves in the CC e⁻ plots show this.
  • Cutoff independence. For CC e⁻, DISENT cutoffs 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻¹⁰ agree for ρE and for τzE, BzE with the cut (χ² 28.6, 27.6, 15.3 for 24 bins). The uncut τzE and BzE change by 15–60%.
  • Fluctuation check. The largest χ², NC Z ρE (36.8 / 24), was rerun with 30 new seeds in both codes. New NNLOJET against new disorder gives 20.3 / 24, and the combined samples 27.0 / 24. The two disorder samples agree (20.0 / 24), while the two NNLOJET samples differ from each other by 36.7 / 24 (pulls up to 2.7 at ρ ≈ 0.13–0.22). So the excess came from the NNLOJET sample, not from disorder.
  • Not tested externally. NNLOJET has no e⁺ NC and no neutrino beams, so NC e⁺, NC ν/ν and CC ν/ν have no O(αs²) reference. Their matrix elements use the same structures with other coupling values: e⁺ flips C3, and ν/ν change C2 and C3. The couplings are validated at LO and O(αs), and the CC runs cover both signs of C3 and the W-specific interference classes.
